# Prototype review

## Three Ages of a Brussels Building

The interaction makes the candidate's identity clear: the product is an evidence-led building-history explorer. The important design choice is to show disagreement and uncertainty rather than collapse all information into one “true” construction year.

A first public source is now available: the City of Brussels dataset describing 34 Grand Place buildings, including restoration/history and facade-description fields. Its catalogue states CC BY 4.0 with the City publisher plus `Behind Brussels` and `Google Maps` attributions; the architectural-inventory terms separately permit text quotations and information reuse with explicit source attribution rather than granting a general image licence. The committed snapshot now preserves its public dataset endpoint. Four aligned ortho previews now cover the pilot area: BruCiel 1930–1935, 1971 and 1996 under CC0, plus the open-data 2022 urbisgrid ortho with attribution recorded. Eight KIK-IRPA BALaT facade photographs are committed under CC BY 4.0: five from 1941–1942, 1969 and 1984 views of Joseph et Anne, and an 1890 west-side ensemble view awaiting identity confirmation. Maison de la Balance has two exact-case facade epochs: Victor Dedoncker's 1878 engraving from a British Library institutional scan marked no known copyright restrictions, and a 2011 Wikimedia Commons photograph under CC BY-SA 3.0. Every pilot case has reusable case-level imagery, La Balance has a historical/modern facade pair, Joseph et Anne has three historical facade epochs, and the area has a four-epoch structural source run. All fourteen committed source previews are bound to pilot provenance and downloader expectations by SHA-256. Twenty-four deterministic building-centred crops apply identical boxes across the four structural epochs and are bound to the review worksheet by source checksum and crop-pixel hash. The 1944 BruCiel layer remains a retired cascade, but it is no longer the access blocker; review of visible changes is.

The 34 source records all contain history text and 32 contain facade-description text. A six-record source-linked pilot now separates source-described facade style, reconstruction proxies, register evidence and reusable image previews. Three cases retain heritage-register-linked Wikidata proxies; their structured claims are reusable under CC0 1.0 but remain crowd-sourced values. The other three now have direct Brussels architectural heritage inventory reconstruction-date proxies (1697, 1704 and 1697). These are official source dates, but not structured original-construction-year fields, so they remain explicitly unreviewed proxies. A six-row provenance-safe worksheet now exposes each claim’s actual semantics and agreement state and limits review to three controlled outcomes. The La Balance case also exposes a source-identity discrepancy: the City dataset says `Grand-Place 24`; heritage inventory Urban 30991 says `Rue de la Colline 24` but treats the facade as part of the Grand-Place ensemble; the British Library caption says `rue de la Colline`; and Commons says `Grand-Place` with protected-monument identifier `2043-0177/0`. These labels and identifiers are now an explicit four-source crosswalk rather than a claim that Commons supplies the heritage street address. **Still needed:** reviewer confirmation of that identity/address mapping, case-level interpretation of the four-epoch structural comparison, six controlled register decisions and reviewed labels.

## Current conclusion

The product shape and bounded source access are demonstrated. The remaining feasibility bottleneck is reviewer capacity and label validity: confirm the La Balance identity crosswalk, interpret case-level structural comparisons, record controlled register decisions and review facade labels before presenting a curated chronology.
